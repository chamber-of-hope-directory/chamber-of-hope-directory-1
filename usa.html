📱 What to Ask 211

Call 211 or start at 211.org. Tell them your ZIP code first, then your most urgent need.

  • Simple script: “Hi, I live in [ZIP / city]. I need help with housing, food, medical care, transportation, legal help, or bills. What can I apply for first?”
  • Ask next: “Do you have same-day help, shelter tonight, food today, or emergency utility help?”
  • If you are denied: “Is there a waitlist, alternate program, or partner agency I should call next?”
  • Good things to request: housing referrals, food pantries, utility help, transportation, clinics, legal aid, domestic violence resources, and local DSS / Human Services contacts.

🧭 If You Were Denied, Waitlisted, or Could Not Reach Anyone

  1. Ask for the reason, decision notice, deadline, and appeal instructions in writing.
  2. Ask whether funding is temporarily closed, whether a waitlist exists, and when to check again.
  3. Request an alternate program, partner agency, supervisor, interpreter, or disability accommodation when needed.
  4. Save names, dates, case numbers, screenshots, letters, and confirmation numbers.
  5. For civil legal help with benefits, housing, or another denial, use USAGov legal-aid guidance or LawHelp.org.
  6. For unresolved concerns involving a federal agency, review USAGov federal-agency complaint guidance.

Appeal rules and deadlines vary by program. Follow the instructions on the official decision notice first.

🛡️ Scam & Identity-Theft Recovery

  • Use official .gov websites, trusted nonprofits, and established hotlines.
  • Never pay upfront fees for “guaranteed” housing, faster benefits, waitlist placement, grants, or immigration forms.
  • Identity theft: use IdentityTheft.gov for a personal recovery plan, reporting steps, and sample letters.
  • Fraud or impersonation scam: report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and contact your bank or payment service immediately if money was sent.
  • Lost, stolen, or exposed information: change affected passwords, preserve messages and receipts, review accounts, and follow the steps at IdentityTheft.gov.
  • Immigration legal help: use USCIS scam warnings and official legal-help directories only.
