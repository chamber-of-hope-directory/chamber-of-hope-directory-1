📋 Before You Contact a Provider

  • Write down the exact help you need, your city or area, household size, and any deadline.
  • Keep any eviction, shutoff, denial, medical, school, court, or appointment notice nearby.
  • Ask whether the program is open, who qualifies, which documents are required, and whether an appointment is needed.
  • Tell the provider early if you need an interpreter, accessible communication, mobility access, sensory accommodations, or help completing forms.
  • Before traveling, confirm the address, hours, transportation options, fees, and whether walk-ins are accepted.

📞 How to Ask for Help

  • Call script: “Hi, I need help with housing, food, medical care, legal help, or another basic need. Can you tell me what programs or referrals are available?”
  • Email script: “Hello, I am looking for help with ______. I live in ______. Can you tell me what support, referrals, or next steps may be available?”
  • If you are denied: ask, “Is there a waitlist, appeal, alternate program, or partner agency I should contact next?”

🧾 What to Gather First

  • Photo ID or any form of identity
  • Proof of address or where you are staying
  • Income, pay stubs, or benefits letters if you have them
  • Children’s documents, school papers, or medical papers if relevant
  • Phone numbers, case numbers, appointment letters, and prescriptions if relevant

🚪 What to Do When Help Says No or Places You on a Waitlist

  1. Ask for the reason and the eligibility rule in writing when possible.
  2. Ask whether there is an appeal, reconsideration, supervisor review, or missing-document process.
  3. Ask when funding or enrollment may reopen and whether there is a cancellation or priority list.
  4. Request another program, partner agency, local office, or statewide referral.
  5. Ask for an interpreter or reasonable accommodation if communication or accessibility affected the application.
  6. Record the date, staff name, telephone number, reference number, and next step.
  7. Try a different pathway in the state, city, country, USA National, or Global HELPS section.

⏱️ First 24 Hours Checklist

  • Housing: ask about shelter, motel vouchers, rapid rehousing, and eviction prevention.
  • Food: ask about food pantries, same-day meals, SNAP/WIC, and delivery help if you cannot travel.
  • Medical: ask about urgent care, free/low-cost clinics, prescriptions, and transportation help.
  • Safety: if the need is urgent or dangerous, contact emergency or crisis services right away.

🛡️ How to Avoid Scams

  • FTC ReportFraud: reportfraud.ftc.gov
  • IdentityTheft.gov: identitytheft.gov
  • IC3 Internet Crime Complaint Center: ic3.gov
  • Do not pay upfront fees for guaranteed housing, immigration help, or benefits help.
  • Use official websites, verified hotlines, and trusted local agencies whenever possible.

🗣️ Communication & Self-Advocacy Tools

  • Prepare a one-page note stating: “I need help with…,” preferred communication method, important accommodations, and the best person to contact.
  • Use plain language, pictures, a communication device, written choices, an interpreter, or a trusted support person when helpful.
  • Ask staff to repeat or write down eligibility rules, deadlines, decisions, and next steps.
  • State clearly when you need extra time, accessible forms, mobility access, sensory support, or another reasonable accommodation.
  • Keep a record of names, dates, reference numbers, and what was agreed upon.

♿ Assistive Technology & Medical Equipment Planning

  • Start with the task or safety problem the equipment must solve rather than a brand name.
  • Ask whether an assessment, prescription, therapist recommendation, or insurance authorization is required.
  • Ask about demonstrations, trial periods, training, fitting, delivery, maintenance, repairs, batteries, warranties, and replacement supplies.
  • Compare purchase, rental, loan, reuse, and funding options before paying.
  • For urgent equipment failure, contact the prescribing provider, supplier, insurer, emergency plan contact, or local medical service as appropriate.

🦷 Special-Needs Dental Visit Guidance

  • Ask whether the office serves patients with developmental, sensory, mobility, communication, or complex medical needs.
  • Explain helpful routines, triggers, communication methods, positioning needs, and the best time of day for the visit.
  • Ask about wheelchair access, quiet waiting, gradual visits, caregiver participation, and extra appointment time.
  • Discuss medical history, medications, allergies, sedation options, consent, insurance, and expected costs before treatment.
  • Request written after-care instructions in an accessible format.

🐾 Pet Crisis & Evacuation Planning

  • Prepare a carrier or leash, identification, food, water, medications, waste supplies, and vaccination records.
  • Keep a recent photo and confirm that identification tags or microchip information are current.
  • Name a temporary caregiver and identify pet-friendly shelters, hotels, boarding, or rescue options before an emergency.
  • Ask housing, shelter, hospital, or transportation programs about service animals, emotional-support animals, and household pets before arrival.
  • Never leave a pet behind in a dangerous location when safe evacuation help is available.

🔐 Digital Safety & Privacy

  • Use official websites and confirm the web address before entering personal information.
  • Use strong, different passwords and turn on two-step verification when available.
  • Share Social Security, banking, medical, immigration, or identity documents only when necessary and through a trusted method.
  • Avoid sending sensitive documents over public Wi-Fi or to an unverified social-media account.
  • Save confirmation numbers and screenshots, then remove sensitive files from shared devices when finished.

🏠 Home-Safety Planning

  • Check exits, smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms, lighting, loose rugs, stairs, rails, and clear walking paths.
  • Secure medications, cleaning products, sharp items, firearms, matches, and unsafe appliances as appropriate.
  • Plan for safe water temperature, bathroom transfers, cooking, laundry, and nighttime supervision needs.
  • Keep emergency contacts, medical information, flashlights, chargers, and essential supplies easy to find.
  • If someone depends on powered medical equipment, make a power-outage and evacuation plan.

🫶 Caregiver Planning & Backup Support

  • Keep a short care summary with diagnoses or support needs, routines, communication preferences, allergies, medications, and emergency contacts.
  • List what the person can do independently, what requires prompting, and what requires hands-on or safety support.
  • Identify at least one backup caregiver and explain where essential supplies and documents are kept.
  • Include transportation, mobility, sensory, behavior, dietary, and medical-equipment needs.
  • Keep copies of guardianship, consent, insurance, waiver, or service-plan documents when applicable, and review the plan regularly.
