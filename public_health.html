  • For local alerts, check your city or county health department and local emergency management office.

How to Help Prevent the Spread of Illness

Simple prevention steps can help reduce the spread of respiratory illness, stomach illness, foodborne illness, and other contagious conditions.

  • Stay home when you are sick, especially with fever, vomiting, diarrhea, coughing, or other contagious symptoms.
  • Wash hands often with soap and water for at least 20 seconds.
  • Use soap and water, especially after using the restroom, changing diapers, cleaning up vomit or diarrhea, and before preparing food.
  • Hand sanitizer can help for some germs, but it does not work well for every illness.
  • Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or your elbow, then wash your hands.
  • Avoid close contact with others when sick or recently exposed to illness.
  • Wear a mask when sick, recovering, exposed, in crowded indoor spaces, or around people at higher risk.
  • Improve airflow when possible by opening windows, using fans safely, using air filtration, or spending time outdoors when appropriate.
  • Clean and disinfect frequently touched surfaces.
  • Wash laundry carefully if clothing, towels, or bedding have vomit or diarrhea on them.
  • Do not prepare food for others while sick with vomiting or diarrhea.
  • Stay up to date on vaccines recommended by your doctor or local health department.

Food Safety & Illness Prevention Note

Foodborne illness alerts can change quickly. Always check official updates before deciding what foods to avoid.

  • Avoid foods listed in an active CDC, FDA, state, or local health department alert.
  • Do not eat food that has been recalled, even if it looks or smells normal.
  • If a recalled food is in your home, throw it away or return it to the store.
  • Clean and sanitize any shelves, drawers, containers, counters, utensils, or surfaces the recalled food touched.
  • Wash hands with soap and water before and after handling fruits and vegetables.
  • Wash fresh fruits and vegetables under running water before eating, cutting, peeling, or cooking.
  • Scrub firm produce, such as melons, cucumbers, potatoes, and carrots, with a clean produce brush.
  • Remove damaged or bruised areas before preparing produce.
  • Keep cut, peeled, or cooked fruits and vegetables refrigerated.
  • Keep raw meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs away from ready-to-eat foods.
  • Cook food to a safe temperature when cooking is appropriate.
  • Refrigerate perishable food within 2 hours, or within 1 hour if food has been in heat above 90°F.
  • Do not use soap, bleach, detergent, disinfectant, or household cleaners on fruits and vegetables.
  • Washing can reduce dirt and germs, but it may not remove every parasite or bacteria. Cooking food when appropriate gives stronger protection.

Current Public Health Note: Severe Watery Diarrhea / Cyclospora

Review date: August 3, 2026

Health officials are monitoring cyclosporiasis, an intestinal illness caused by a parasite called Cyclospora. Symptoms may include frequent watery diarrhea, stomach cramps, bloating, nausea, loss of appetite, fatigue, and dehydration.

Cyclospora is often linked to contaminated food or water. It is not usually spread directly from person to person.

People with frequent or severe diarrhea should stay hydrated and contact a health care provider, especially if symptoms last more than a few days, return after improving, or happen after eating food connected to a public health alert.

Check before sharing: Use the official CDC, FDA, state, or local health department page for the most current outbreak information.

When to Seek Medical Help

Seek urgent medical help or call emergency services for:

  • Trouble breathing
  • Chest pain
  • Confusion
  • Severe dehydration
  • Blood in stool
  • Severe stomach pain
  • High fever that will not improve
  • Symptoms in infants, older adults, pregnant people, or people with weakened immune systems
  • Diarrhea, vomiting, or illness that lasts more than a few days or keeps returning
