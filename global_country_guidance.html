Start Here: Country First, Then Global

Most help is controlled by the country, territory, province, state, city, ministry, court, health office, or local agency where the person lives or where the event happened. Use this page to choose the right starting point without assuming U.S. terms apply everywhere.

  1. Choose the country or territory. Start with the official government website or the country page in this directory.
  2. Choose the need. Benefits, health coverage, records, safety, disaster help, legal aid, consumer protection, disability support, child/family help, housing, or migration/refugee support.
  3. Use official sources first. Government offices, ministries, courts, national agencies, national meteorological/disaster agencies, embassies, consulates, and recognized humanitarian organizations should confirm the next step.
  4. Use global sources when local information is unclear. Global sources can help identify official pathways, country systems, alerts, humanitarian reports, or recognized aid organizations.

Important: Chamber of Hope shares country-safe pathways and official starting points only. It does not provide immigration advice, legal advice, medical advice, financial advice, insurance advice, tax advice, emergency response, case management, professional approval, or guaranteed services.

Country Pathways: What Office Should Someone Look For?

Benefits, pensions & social protection

Look for the country’s social security office, pension agency, benefits ministry, labor ministry, disability office, child/family benefit office, survivor benefit office, unemployment office, or social welfare office.

Health coverage & public health

Look for the health ministry, national health insurance agency, public health department, local clinic network, hospital financial-help office, prescription/medicine program, disability health office, or public health alert page.

Records, archives & civil documents

Look for civil registry, vital records, births/deaths/marriages office, local municipality, court, land registry, national archives, parish/church register, cemetery office, embassy, or consulate.

Legal aid & consumer protection

Look for legal aid, public defender, ombudsman, consumer protection agency, insurance regulator, financial regulator, labor rights office, court self-help center, or government complaint office.

Emergency, disaster & weather safety

Look for the country’s emergency number, national meteorological agency, disaster management agency, civil protection office, health emergency alerts, evacuation/shelter notices, utility providers, and local officials.

Embassy, consulate & citizens abroad

For citizens outside their home country, check the nearest embassy or consulate for passport, emergency, death abroad, arrest, hospitalization, evacuation guidance, document, notarial, and repatriation pathways.

Refugees, asylum seekers & displaced people

Look for the host country’s asylum/refugee office, UNHCR help page, recognized refugee-support organizations, local legal aid, child protection, health services, and community-based assistance.

Children, families, disability & caregivers

Look for child protection, education disability support, social welfare, family benefit offices, disability councils, independent living organizations, caregiver support, health ministry, and legal aid.

Do not assume that Medicare, Medicaid, Social Security, SSI, 401(k), Roth IRA, or U.S. vital records offices apply outside U.S. systems unless the person is specifically handling U.S.-based benefits or records.

Safety, Accuracy & Scam Protection

  • Use official country websites and recognized organizations before sharing personal information.
  • Be careful with third-party sites that imitate government pages or charge unnecessary fees.
  • Do not send identity documents, benefit numbers, passport numbers, bank information, or login details through social media messages or unknown links.
  • For emergencies, use the local emergency number and local official instructions where the person is physically located.
  • For immigration, asylum, legal status, inheritance, adoption, custody, pension transfer, tax, or insurance decisions, contact the official agency or a qualified professional in that country.

📘 International Education Records, Transcripts & Credentials

For international school records, diplomas, certificates, trade/vocational records, college or university transcripts, professional training, apprenticeships, or credential recognition, start with the school/university registrar, Ministry of Education, national qualifications authority, examination board, professional licensing board, embassy, consulate, or official country records office.

Open International / Country Education Records Guide

Do not assume U.S. transcript, diploma, GED, or college-credit rules apply globally. The receiving school, employer, licensing board, immigration office, or credential evaluator should confirm what exact record format is accepted.

Personal, Medical, Student Clinic, Legal, Property & Service Records

For records outside the U.S., start with the country or territory office, provider, clinic, student clinic, school/program, court, health authority, justice agency, police/justice office, motor vehicle or transport agency, housing/rental authority, land registry, utility regulator, communications regulator, employer, embassy, consulate, or official portal connected to the record.
