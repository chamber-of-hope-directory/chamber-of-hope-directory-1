Urgent vs. Non-Urgent Help

Use emergency or crisis help now for:

  • Immediate danger, violence, fire, serious injury, or medical emergency
  • No safe place to stay tonight
  • Suicidal crisis, severe emotional distress, or danger to self/others
  • Active abuse, neglect, trafficking, stalking, or unsafe escape needs
  • Evacuation, flooding, severe weather, power danger, or disaster instructions

Use regular help pathways for:

  • Food, housing, utilities, benefits, insurance, records, transcripts, or planning questions
  • Retirement, disability, caregiver, life-stage, and future-planning needs
  • Appointments, referrals, applications, waitlists, or follow-up calls
  • General resource searching when there is no immediate safety danger

Immediate danger: contact the emergency service for the current location. In the United States, dial 911. For suicidal crisis or emotional distress in the United States and U.S. territories, call or text 988.

Before You Call Checklist

Have what you can. Do not stop yourself from calling just because you do not have every document.

  • Full name, date of birth, phone number, email, and current address or location
  • Household size, ages of children, caregiver status, disability needs, or senior needs if relevant
  • Photo ID, school ID, benefit card, insurance card, Medicaid/Medicare card, or other ID if available
  • Proof of income, benefit letters, pay stubs, unemployment papers, tax papers, or bank statement if requested
  • Lease, eviction notice, utility shutoff notice, medical bill, prescription information, or other proof of need
  • Case numbers, appointment letters, denial letters, waitlist notices, or names of agencies already contacted
  • School records, IEP/504 papers, transcripts, custody/guardianship papers, or caregiver authorization if relevant
  • A notebook or phone note to write down who you contacted, what they said, and the next step

What to Say When Asking for Help

Use these simple words if you feel nervous, overwhelmed, or unsure how to begin.

Phone script

“Hello, my name is _____. I live in _____. I need help with _____. Can you tell me if your program helps with this, what documents I need, and what my next step should be?”

Email or message script

“Hello, I am looking for help with _____. I live in _____. Can you tell me if your organization provides help, referrals, a waitlist, or another place I should contact?”

Voicemail script

“Hello, my name is _____. My phone number is _____. I am calling about help with _____. Please call me back and let me know the next step. Thank you.”

If You Are Denied, Waitlisted, or Turned Away

  • Ask why the program cannot help right now.
  • Ask whether there is an appeal, review, reconsideration, or another application step.
  • Ask whether a written notice or reference number is available.
  • Ask if there is a waitlist and how often you should check back.
  • Ask for another program, partner agency, hotline, church, nonprofit, or local government office to contact.
  • Ask whether emergency help, same-day help, or a crisis pathway exists for the situation.
  • Write down the date, person/office name, phone number, and what was said.
  • If the issue involves benefits, housing, safety, discrimination, legal papers, or rights, consider legal aid or an official appeals office.
  • Try more than one starting point. One “no” does not mean help does not exist.

For Caregivers, Guardians, Parents, and Family Helpers

If you are helping someone else, ask what permission or paperwork is needed before an agency can speak with you.

  • Ask whether the person must be present or give verbal/written permission.
  • Bring guardianship papers, power of attorney, representative payee papers, custody papers, caregiver authorization, or consent forms if applicable.
  • For medical needs, ask whether a HIPAA release or patient authorization is needed.
  • For school records, ask whether parent/guardian rights, student consent, FERPA rules, or school release forms apply.
  • For disability or aging support, ask about accommodations, accessible formats, caregiver supports, and long-term service options.
  • Write down the person’s needs clearly: communication, mobility, vision, hearing, behavior, medical, dietary, transportation, or safety support.

Language & Disability Access Reminder

  • Ask if interpretation is available in your language.
  • Ask for translated documents, plain-language help, or help completing forms.
  • Ask for disability accommodations, accessible formats, large print, TTY, relay service, captioning, mobility access, or communication support.
  • If calling for someone who is nonverbal, Deaf, hard of hearing, blind/low vision, intellectually disabled, autistic, or medically fragile, explain what communication support is needed.

Scam & Safe Contact Reminder

  • Do not give Social Security, Medicare, bank, insurance, passport, or benefit-login information to unexpected callers, texts, emails, or social-media messages.
  • Do not pay upfront fees to apply for public benefits.
  • Use official websites, official phone numbers, known agencies, and trusted local offices.
  • Be careful with pressure words like “act now,” “guaranteed approval,” “benefits canceled,” “free money,” or “send payment first.”
  • If unsure, hang up and call the official agency directly using a verified number.
