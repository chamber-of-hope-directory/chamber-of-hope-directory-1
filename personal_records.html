Start Here: Who Created the Record?

Most records are held by the office, agency, provider, clinic, student clinic, school/program, training site, court, jail/prison, lender, property office, utility company, phone/cable provider, employer, or country/territory system that created the record.

Best first step: contact the record holder directly and ask for the records, forms, proof of identity, fees, processing time, and whether the record can be mailed, emailed, picked up, or sent to another official office.

What to Gather Before Requesting Records

Basic information

  • Full name used at the time
  • Date of birth or account/case number if known
  • Date or approximate date of service, case, account, property, or event
  • City, county, state, territory, or country connected to the record
  • Current contact information and mailing address

Proof and permission

  • Photo ID or official account login when required
  • Proof of relationship or legal authority if requesting for someone else
  • Signed release or authorization form when needed
  • Court order, guardianship, power of attorney, executor papers, or representative paperwork when required
  • Payment method if an official fee applies

Request details

  • What records you need and date range
  • Whether you need a certified, official, complete, itemized, or billing record
  • Where the record should be sent
  • Deadline or reason for the request
  • Notes from any office already contacted

Hospital, Clinic, Health Department & Immunization Records

Hospital or clinic records

Start with the hospital, clinic, doctor’s office, dental office, pharmacy, lab, health plan, patient portal, or medical records / Health Information Management office. Ask for a release-of-information form, date range, type of record, processing time, fees, and how records can be sent.

Shot / immunization records

Start with the doctor, clinic, pharmacy, school, employer health office, military record, local health department, or state/territory immunization registry. CDC does not keep personal vaccine records, so the provider or immunization registry is usually the better starting point.

International health records

Outside the U.S., start with the hospital, clinic, vaccination provider, local health authority, Ministry of Health, national health system, private insurer, embassy/consulate guidance, or official health record portal where available.

Student Clinic, Clinical Training & Learner Practice Records

Use this when services, care, teaching, evaluations, or practice work were completed through a student clinic, school program, training program, practicum, internship, rotation, field placement, service-learning activity, or supervised learner setting.

Patient file, EHR, SOAP notes and encounter notes

Start with the student clinic, hospital, clinic, doctor’s office, therapy office, dental office, patient portal, medical records / Health Information Management office, or release-of-information office. Ask for the patient file, electronic health record, visit summary, SOAP notes, clinical encounter notes, treatment plan, consent forms, lab/imaging records, referrals, discharge notes, and billing records for the dates needed.

Dental chart and student dental clinic records

Start with the dental school clinic, student dental clinic, dental records office, patient services office, provider office, or clinical affairs office. Ask for the dental chart, chart notes, x-rays/images, treatment plan, procedure notes, consent forms, prescriptions, referrals, payments, and follow-up instructions.

Assessment rubrics, learner feedback and placement records

Start with the school, college, university, program office, registrar, placement office, clinical coordinator, field education office, student-teaching office, site supervisor, or training provider. Ask for the assessment rubric, learner feedback form, evaluation, practicum or rotation dates, hours completed, service verification, attendance record, certificate, or letter of completion. Some learner records may require the student’s written permission.

All types of student practice

For student teaching, nursing, medical, dental, therapy, counseling, social work, legal clinic, cosmetology/barber, veterinary, trade, apprenticeship, community service, or similar supervised practice records, start with the program and the site where the service occurred. Ask who controls the record and whether it is treated as a patient record, education record, training record, employment record, or service record.

Legal, Court, Juvenile & Incarceration Records

Court and legal records

Start with the court clerk, courthouse, case portal, legal aid, public defender, attorney, prosecutor’s office, probate court, family court, or records office where the case was filed. Federal court records may be available through PACER or the federal courthouse clerk.

Juvenile records

Juvenile records are often restricted, confidential, sealed, or handled differently from adult records. Start with the juvenile court, court services unit, child welfare agency, probation office, attorney, legal aid, or state/territory juvenile justice agency. Ask what proof or court order is required.

Incarceration / detention records

Start with the jail, sheriff, state department of corrections, prison records office, federal Bureau of Prisons, probation/parole office, immigration detention agency, or court connected to the case. For international matters, begin with the country’s justice ministry, prison service, court, legal aid, embassy, or consulate.

Criminal Records, Police Records & Background Checks

Criminal or police records

Start with the court clerk, state police or criminal justice agency, local police department, sheriff, prosecutor, public defender, probation/parole office, corrections agency, or official record portal connected to the case or arrest. Ask whether the record is public, restricted, sealed, expunged, juvenile, or requires ID, fingerprints, a signed release, or a court order.

Background checks

Ask what type of background check is needed: personal review, employment, rental housing, volunteer service, licensing, caregiving, school placement, immigration, travel, or international police clearance. Use the official agency or approved process requested by the receiving office.

If a report affects housing or work

If a landlord or employer uses a background-check company and the report affects an application, ask for the name of the reporting company, a copy of the report when allowed, and dispute steps for inaccurate information. Rules vary by location and by type of check.

International police clearance

For records outside the U.S., start with the national police, justice ministry, court, local police station, embassy, consulate, or official government police-certificate process. Ask whether the record needs fingerprints, translation, apostille, authentication, or direct submission.

Auto, Driver, Vehicle & DMV Records

Driver and DMV records

Start with the state, territory, provincial, or country motor vehicle agency where the license, registration, title, ticket, crash report, or vehicle record was created. Ask about driving records, title records, registration history, license status, accident/crash reports, fines, liens, reinstatement notices, and copy fees.

Vehicle title, VIN, repair, and insurance records

For vehicle history, begin with the motor vehicle agency, title office, insurer, repair shop, dealer, finance company, lienholder, tow yard, police report, or approved vehicle-history source. Ask for the VIN, title/brand history, odometer information, lien release, repair invoices, claim records, or impound/tow records.

International and territory vehicle records

For U.S. territories and other countries, start with the territory DMV or country transport authority, vehicle registry, road-safety agency, insurance regulator, police traffic unit, or local municipality. Access rules and required proof can vary widely.

Rental, Tenant, Lease & Housing Records

Rental and lease records

Start with the landlord, property manager, housing authority, rental portal, tenant ledger, or lease office. Ask for the lease, renewal papers, rent ledger, payment receipts, security deposit records, move-in/move-out inspection, maintenance requests, notices, emails, and written account history.

Tenant screening and background reports

If a rental application was denied or affected by a tenant screening/background report, ask for the screening company name, the notice, the report copy when available, and how to dispute incorrect information.

Eviction or housing court records

Start with the court clerk, housing court, legal aid, landlord-tenant office, or local tenant-help program where the case was filed. Ask whether records are public, sealed, dismissed, satisfied, or require a court request to correct or restrict.

Mortgage, Property, Landlord, Housing & Property Records

Mortgage records

Start with the mortgage servicer, lender, loan portal, closing company, title company, escrow office, or housing counselor. Ask for payment history, escrow records, payoff statements, servicing file, notices, modification records, or dispute instructions.

Property and land records

Start with the county/city recorder, clerk, register of deeds, land registry, tax assessor, property appraiser, treasurer, title office, or local land records system where the property is located. Ask about deeds, liens, tax records, parcel records, maps, and copies.

International / territory property records

For U.S. territories and other countries, start with the territory land office, tax office, recorder, cadastre, land registry, property authority, local municipality, notary office, legal aid, embassy, or consulate guidance. Property rules and access can vary by country.

Utility, Phone, Cable, Internet & Service Records

Utility records

Start with the water, sewer, electric, gas, trash, heating, cooling, or municipal utility provider. Ask for billing history, payment history, shutoff notice, service address, account notes, hardship records, outage credits, dispute steps, or assistance program paperwork.

Phone, cable, internet and TV records

Start with the provider’s online account, customer service, billing department, retention/collections department, or records request process. Ask for bills, contracts, service dates, equipment return records, outage records, payment history, call/chat notes, or dispute/complaint records.

When there is a dispute

If the provider does not resolve a billing or service issue, ask for a supervisor, dispute address, complaint number, and written response. In the U.S., FCC may help with communication-service complaints, and state public utility commissions may handle some utility issues.

Work, Training, Student-Teacher, Volunteer & Placement Records

For employment, internship, student-teacher placement, apprenticeship, volunteer, training, certification, clinical rotation, field placement, student-practice, or service-hour records, start with the employer, HR office, school district, college/university placement office, training provider, apprenticeship sponsor, licensing board, certification agency, volunteer coordinator, clinical coordinator, site supervisor, or program administrator.

Ask for the record type needed, such as dates served, hours completed, evaluation forms, assessment rubrics, learner feedback forms, background-check confirmation, practicum/student-teaching/service-learning verification, rotation verification, training certificate, wage record, separation record, or letter of verification.

U.S. Territories, Countries & International Seekers

U.S. territories

For Puerto Rico, U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa, and Northern Mariana Islands / CNMI, start with the territory office, provider, school/program, clinic, court, or agency that created or regulates the record: health department, education department, student clinic, licensing board, court, corrections agency, land/property office, utility regulator, consumer protection office, or official territory portal.

International countries

For records outside the U.S., start with the country’s official agency or record holder: Ministry of Health, national health system, hospital/clinic, dental clinic, civil registry, court, justice ministry, prison/corrections service, land registry, utility regulator, communications regulator, school/university, training program, employer, embassy, consulate, or local legal aid.

Translations and official use

If a record will be used for school, immigration, court, benefits, licensing, work, housing, or another country, ask the receiving office whether it must be official, certified, translated, notarized, apostilled, authenticated, sealed, or sent directly.

Privacy, Safety & Scam Protection

  • Use official provider, clinic, student clinic, agency, court, school/program, lender, utility, phone/cable, territory, country, or government websites first.
  • Be careful with third-party sites that promise instant records, secret records, guaranteed background checks, guaranteed criminal-record removal, guaranteed deletions, fake expungement, fake benefits, or guaranteed document retrieval.
  • Do not send IDs, Social Security numbers, passport numbers, account numbers, medical information, school records, court documents, or bank details through unknown links.
  • For restricted, sealed, juvenile, adoption, custody, criminal, immigration, or property disputes, consider legal aid or a qualified attorney.
  • For urgent danger, medical emergencies, abuse, threats, or unsafe housing conditions, contact emergency services or the appropriate local authority for your location.

Simple Request Script

“Hello, my name is ______. I am trying to request records for ______. The record may be under the name ______ and connected to ______ location/date/account/case/VIN/lease/report number, clinic visit, training site, program, rotation, or service date. Can you tell me the correct form, ID needed, fee, processing time, and whether the records can be sent to me or directly to another office?”

Where This Records Help Applies

This page is for U.S. seekers, U.S. territory seekers, international country seekers, and global visitors who need to know where to begin. Rules, fees, privacy limits, forms, language access, and proof requirements may be different by location, so always start with the official office or provider that created or controls the record.
