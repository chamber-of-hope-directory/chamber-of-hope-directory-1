What to Gather Before You Call or Apply

  • Photo ID or another identity document.
  • Social Security number, benefit letters, Medicare/Medicaid cards, or other official benefit documents, if applicable.
  • Birth certificate or proof of age; marriage, divorce, custody, guardianship, adoption, or death records if relevant.
  • Proof of income, tax papers, bank information, employer names, work history, or unemployment records.
  • Health insurance cards, doctor/medication lists, disability records, IEP/504 paperwork, medical equipment needs, or caregiver notes.
  • Pension, 401(k), 403(b), 457, IRA, Roth IRA, annuity, life insurance, or old-employer benefit paperwork.
  • Beneficiary information, power of attorney, representative payee, guardianship, supported decision-making, trust, or estate documents if already in place.
  • Important rule: do not give login codes, bank details, Medicare numbers, Social Security numbers, or insurance information to unexpected callers, texts, emails, or social-media messages.

Disability Benefits, Insurance & Future Planning Help

People with disabilities may need help with income, health coverage, long-term services, work support, transportation, assistive technology, housing, caregiver support, school transition, legal planning, and safe financial planning.

Important pathways to check:

  • SSI: may help adults and children with disability, blindness, limited income, and limited resources.
  • SSDI: may help people whose disability limits or stops work and who meet work-credit rules.
  • Disabled Adult Child / survivor-related benefits: ask SSA if disability began before age 22 or if benefits may connect to a parent’s work record.
  • Medicare before 65: ask Medicare/SSA about disability-based Medicare timing and exceptions.
  • Medicaid and waivers: check state Medicaid, local disability agencies, and Aging & Disability Resource Centers for long-term services and supports.
  • ABLE accounts: can help eligible people with disabilities save for qualified disability expenses; ask a qualified tax/benefits professional how it affects your situation.
  • Special needs trusts, guardianship, supported decision-making, representative payee, and power of attorney: ask legal aid or a qualified attorney before signing documents.
  • Independent living and caregiver help: check DIAL, Centers for Independent Living, ADRCs, vocational rehabilitation, and local disability organizations.

Older Adults, Retirement & Social Security

Retirement timing affects income, health coverage, taxes, work decisions, spouse or survivor benefits, and long-term planning. The directory should guide people to official tools and qualified helpers, not decide for them.

Questions to ask before collecting Social Security:

  • What is my estimated monthly benefit at age 62, full retirement age, and age 70?
  • How will working while receiving benefits affect me?
  • Do spouse, divorced spouse, widow/widower, survivor, or dependent benefits apply?
  • How will Medicare enrollment, employer insurance, COBRA, Marketplace insurance, Medicaid, or VA benefits affect my decision?
  • Do I need tax advice before starting benefits or retirement-account withdrawals?
  • Do I have pension, 401(k), IRA, Roth IRA, annuity, or old employer benefits to review?

Survivor, Widow/Widower, Family & End-of-Life Benefits

After a death, families may need help with survivor benefits, life insurance, pension survivor options, funeral and burial help, estate questions, grief support, and legal documents.

  • Ask SSA about survivor benefits for a spouse, divorced spouse, child, dependent parent, or disabled adult child.
  • Contact pension or retirement plan administrators about survivor options.
  • Use NAIC’s policy locator if a life insurance policy or annuity may exist but paperwork is missing.
  • Check funeral/burial help, VA benefits if applicable, probate/legal aid, and grief support.

U.S. Territories Note

Open Clear U.S. Territories Guidance

U.S. territories can have different rules, local agencies, funding structures, Medicaid programs, SSI availability, benefit offices, and health coverage pathways. Territory residents should check Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and territory government offices before assuming the same rule applies everywhere.

Start here for territory residents:

  • Local Social Security office or SSA official website.
  • Medicare official information and SHIP/territory counseling when available.
  • Territory Medicaid / health department.
  • Aging and disability office, legal aid, consumer protection, insurance regulator, and local government benefit office.
  • For American Samoa, Guam, Puerto Rico, U.S. Virgin Islands, and Northern Mariana Islands/CNMI: check the territory-specific Medicaid page and local agency contacts.

Global / Country Benefits, Pension, Disability, Child & Health Coverage Help

Every country handles benefits differently. International pages should not automatically use U.S. terms like Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, 401(k), or Roth IRA unless a person is dealing with U.S. benefits. Use country-specific official government agencies first.

Questions to ask in any country:

  • What retirement, old-age pension, disability, child/family, caregiver, survivor, or unemployment programs exist here?
  • Is health coverage public, private, employer-based, or mixed?
  • Is there help for dental, vision, medicines, assistive devices, or long-term care?
  • Who regulates insurance, pensions, and consumer complaints?
  • If I worked in another country, can my work credits, pension rights, or benefits transfer?
  • If I am an immigrant, refugee, foreign worker, returning citizen, or U.S. citizen abroad, which office or embassy/consulate should I contact?

Scam Protection Reminder

  • Do not share Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, bank, insurance, retirement-account, or login information with unexpected callers, texts, emails, or social-media messages.
  • Be careful with “free Medicare card,” “urgent enrollment,” “benefits canceled,” “guaranteed investment,” “cash bonus,” or “act now” pressure.
  • Use official websites, call official phone numbers directly, and ask a trusted helper before signing insurance, pension, loan, investment, or legal paperwork.
  • Report fraud or suspicious benefits calls to the appropriate official agency.
