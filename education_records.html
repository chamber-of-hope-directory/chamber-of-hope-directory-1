Start Here: What Education Record Do You Need?

Education records are usually held by the school, school district, college/university registrar, trade or career school, state/territory education agency, licensing agency, adult education office, ministry of education, national qualifications office, or another official records custodian.

Best first question: What school or program did the person attend, where was it located, and what record is needed?

What to Gather Before You Request Records

Student details

  • Full legal name used while attending
  • Current name if different
  • Date of birth
  • Student ID number, if known
  • Approximate attendance dates and graduation/completion year
  • School/program name, campus, city, state, territory, or country

Record needed

  • Official transcript, unofficial transcript, diploma, certificate, GED/HSE record, report card, attendance record, discipline record, immunization/school health record, IEP/504/evaluation record, course descriptions, syllabus, or verification of enrollment/completion
  • Number of copies needed
  • Whether the record must be sealed, electronic, mailed directly, translated, notarized, apostilled, or authenticated

Authorization

  • Photo ID or official account login when required
  • Parent/guardian proof for a minor child when allowed
  • Student authorization or release form for adult/postsecondary records
  • Recipient name, school/employer/licensing board, mailing address, email, deadline, and any required form
  • Payment method if the official office charges a fee

U.S. National Pathway

K-12 / elementary / middle / high school

Start with the school office or school district where the student attended. If the school closed or merged, contact the school district, county school office, state education agency, or records custodian listed by the state/territory education department.

GED / high school equivalency

Start with the state or territory adult education agency, official GED/HSE testing office, or the testing service named by that state/territory. Ask for the official transcript or diploma/certificate process.

Special education / IEP / 504 / evaluations

Start with the current or former school district, special education office, student services office, or records office. Ask what records are retained, how long they are kept, and who may request copies.

Trade / vocational / career school

Start with the school registrar or student records office. If the school closed, contact the state licensing agency, state higher education agency, workforce agency, adult education office, or the school’s accreditor/records custodian.

College / university / graduate school

Start with the registrar’s office. Ask whether official transcripts must be sent directly to the receiving school, employer, licensing board, scholarship program, immigration agency, or credential evaluator.

Closed schools

Start with the state licensing agency or higher education agency where the school was located. Ask whether another school, state office, or records custodian stores transcripts. Also check Federal Student Aid closed-school resources when federal aid was involved.

Children, Students With Disabilities & Caregiver Help

  • Parents/guardians may need school records for enrollment, transfer, special education, IEP/504 meetings, disability benefits, medical care, custody/guardianship, or relocation.
  • Students with disabilities may need IEPs, 504 plans, evaluation reports, therapy/service records, accommodation letters, assistive technology records, transition plans, or vocational rehabilitation records.
  • Adult students or college students usually must authorize release of their own education records unless the school says an exception applies.
  • Caregivers, guardians, representative payees, or advocates should ask the school what proof of authority is required before records can be released.

U.S. Territories Education Records

For Puerto Rico, U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa, and Northern Mariana Islands / CNMI, start with the school, school district/education system, college registrar, territory department of education, adult education or GED/HSE office, higher education agency, licensing/workforce agency, or official territory records custodian.

Open U.S. Territories Guidance

Do not assume every state process is the same in every territory. Ask the territory office what records are available, who may request them, and whether certified copies, translations, notarization, apostille, or authentication are needed.

International / Country Education Records

Every country handles school records, diplomas, certificates, apprenticeships, professional training, and university transcripts differently. Start with the school, university registrar, ministry of education, national qualifications authority, examination board, apprenticeship or vocational training authority, professional licensing board, civil registry when education records are tied to identity documents, embassy, consulate, or official credential-recognition office.

International transcripts

Ask whether the record must be official, sealed, mailed directly, digitally verified, translated, notarized, apostilled, legalized, or evaluated by a credential-recognition service.

Credential recognition

For study, work, licensing, immigration, or professional use in another country, ask the receiving school, employer, licensing board, immigration office, or credential-recognition office exactly what version of the record is accepted.

Displaced students / refugees

If records were lost because of conflict, disaster, displacement, school closure, or migration, ask about alternative proof, education history statements, recognized humanitarian education pathways, or qualification-passport programs where available.

Privacy, Safety & Scam Protection

  • Use the official school, district, college, ministry, territory, state, or country website first.
  • Be careful with third-party sites that promise instant official transcripts or guaranteed credential recognition.
  • Do not send Social Security numbers, passport numbers, student ID numbers, banking information, or copies of ID to unknown people or unofficial links.
  • Ask the receiving agency exactly what is required before paying for translations, notarization, apostille, authentication, or credential evaluation.
  • Keep copies of requests, confirmation numbers, payment receipts, tracking numbers, and the name of the office contacted.

Quick Pathway

  1. Identify the school/program and where it was located.
  2. Identify the exact record needed and who needs to receive it.
  3. Start with the school, district, registrar, ministry, state/territory education agency, country education agency, or official records custodian.
  4. Ask whether the record must be official, sealed, electronic, translated, notarized, apostilled, authenticated, or sent directly.
  5. Save every confirmation number, receipt, office name, contact date, and next step.

Reminder: This page gives educational record pathways only. For legal disputes, immigration filings, professional licensing, benefits, custody, disability rights, or credential-recognition decisions, contact the official receiving agency and a qualified professional when needed.
