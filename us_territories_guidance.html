U.S. Territories Guidance

Start here when help is needed in Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands / CNMI, or American Samoa. Territories are U.S.-connected, but benefits, health coverage, local offices, records, and assistance rules may work differently than the 50 states.

Start Here: Choose the Territory First

For territory help, start with the territory where the person lives or where the event happened. Then choose the type of help needed: benefits, healthcare, disability support, child/family support, records, disaster help, insurance questions, legal aid, or consumer protection.

Important: Do not assume that every federal program, enrollment rule, Medicaid option, Medicare help program, SSI rule, or local assistance pathway works the same way in every territory. Check official territory offices and federal sources before making decisions.

Territory Difference Reminder

  • Medicare: Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, and American Samoa are treated as part of the U.S. for Medicare’s “outside the U.S.” travel-coverage definition, but plan access, providers, enrollment help, and drug-cost assistance can differ by location.
  • SSI: SSI rules are not the same across all territories. Check Social Security directly before assuming eligibility.
  • Medicaid / CHIP: Each territory has its own Medicaid/CHIP administration and official contacts. Do not rely on 50-state instructions alone.
  • Marketplace / private health insurance: HealthCare.gov territory pages generally direct residents to territory government offices for Medicaid, CHIP, and other health options.
  • Records: Birth, death, marriage, divorce, court, land, and probate records usually begin with the territory agency, court, health department, registry, archives, or local government office connected to where the event happened.

Helpful Questions to Ask

  • Which territory office handles this benefit, record, or health coverage question?
  • Does this federal program work the same way here, or are territory rules different?
  • Is there a local Medicaid/CHIP office, senior office, disability office, or family benefits office?
  • Is there a territory insurance regulator for health, dental, life, final expense, or other insurance concerns?
  • Who handles birth, death, marriage, divorce, adoption, court, probate, cemetery, land, or archive records?
  • For emergency/disaster needs, which local officials, FEMA office, Red Cross chapter, shelter information, utility updates, or territory emergency agency should be checked?
  • For legal questions, is there legal aid, court self-help, a bar association referral, or consumer protection office?
