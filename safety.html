Emergency Weather & Disaster Safety Updates

Last reviewed: August 7, 2026

Major weather emergencies, natural disasters, power outages, evacuations, and urgent safety concerns can affect people’s ability to find food, shelter, transportation, medical care, safety, and support. Chamber of Hope shares calm pathways and official-source reminders so visitors know where to begin.

How Chamber of Hope uses this section: If a weather or disaster issue is big enough to appear in national or worldwide news and it affects help or safety, it may be mentioned briefly in Weekly Global Help Watch. City-specific storms, road closures, outages, shelters, and local instructions should be checked through local officials, local emergency alerts, utility outage maps, and trusted local news stations.

Current Emergency Weather Watch — Last reviewed: August 7, 2026

Major hazards are active in several parts of the world. GDACS currently shows tropical cyclones in the Pacific, flooding affecting parts of Myanmar, China, India, the Philippines, and the United States, and significant wildfire activity in parts of Greece, Portugal, France, and other regions. What This Section May Cover

  • Severe weather alerts, hurricanes, tropical storms, tornadoes, thunderstorms, flooding, flash flooding, extreme heat, extreme cold, winter storms, wildfire smoke, earthquakes, and other natural disasters.
  • Power outages, evacuations, shelter information, emergency supplies, preparedness, and disaster recovery assistance.
  • Help concerns for older adults, children, people with disabilities, caregivers, pets, people with medical needs, travelers, displaced families, and communities needing urgent support.

Where to Check First

  • National Weather Service for current U.S. watches, warnings, advisories, and weather safety information.
  • National Hurricane Center for Atlantic and eastern Pacific tropical cyclone advisories.
  • NOAA / NWS Weather Prediction Center for excessive rainfall, heat risk, and national hazard outlooks.
  • FEMA / Ready.gov for emergency preparedness and disaster recovery starting points.
  • WMO Severe Weather Information Centre 3.0 for international severe-weather warnings and information from national meteorological and hydrological services.
  • GDACS for global disaster awareness and coordination information for major sudden-onset disasters.
  • Your local city or county emergency management office for evacuation, shelter, road closure, curfew, and boil water notices.
  • Your state, territory, or national emergency management agency for wider emergency updates.
  • Your local utility company outage map for power, gas, water, or service interruptions.
  • Your local public health department when weather or disasters affect health, air quality, water safety, heat safety, or shelter conditions.
  • Trusted local news stations for city-level updates that are confirmed by officials.

Where / How to Help Safely

  • ReliefWeb / OCHA for humanitarian situation reports and recognized response information.
  • IFRC Emergency Appeals for Red Cross / Red Crescent emergency appeals.
  • UNHCR Help for country-specific refugee and displacement help.
  • Use official agencies or recognized local organizations before donating, volunteering, or sharing requests. Do not self-deploy to disaster areas unless an official response agency asks you to.

Emergency Preparedness Reminders

  • Sign up for local emergency alerts when available.
  • Keep phones charged when severe weather is possible.
  • Have flashlights, batteries, water, basic food, medicine, important documents, chargers, and emergency contacts ready.
  • Include pets, medications, assistive devices, mobility equipment, and caregiver needs in emergency planning.
  • Know where to go if local officials tell you to evacuate or shelter in place.
  • Do not drive through floodwater.
  • Avoid downed power lines and report hazards to the proper utility or emergency agency.
  • Check on older adults, people with disabilities, neighbors, caregivers, and anyone who may need extra support.

Immediate Danger

If there is immediate danger, serious injury, trouble breathing, chest pain, fire, flooding inside a home, downed power lines, gas smell, unsafe shelter conditions, or any life-threatening emergency, call emergency services right away.

Chamber of Hope HELPS Directory does not provide emergency response, live weather forecasting, rescue services, medical care, legal advice, or crisis intervention. Always follow emergency services, local officials, the National Weather Service, FEMA, state emergency management, and local emergency management agencies.

Quick Exit and device safety

The Quick Exit button leaves this site for Google. It does not guarantee that browser history or device activity is erased.

  • Use a safer device when possible.
  • Private/incognito browsing may reduce local history but does not hide activity from every network, account, employer, school, or device-monitoring tool.
  • Delete downloads, screenshots, autofill entries, call logs, and messages only when it is safe to do so.
  • Do not make sudden device changes if that could increase danger.
